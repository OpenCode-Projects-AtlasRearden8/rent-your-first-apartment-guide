# Chapter 3: Reading the Lease, Clause by Clause

A lease is less scary than it looks — it's just a list of promises in both directions.
Landlord promises are short; your promises are long, which is why you need to read it.

> **Not legal advice.** Lease terms and roommate/landlord law vary by state and even
> city; some standard clauses are *unenforceable* in certain places even when printed.
> When a clause clashes with something below, the question to ask is usually "what do
> local laws say about this clause?" Legal-aid websites and your state's official
> tenant resources answer this for free. What this chapter can do: teach you to spot
> the clauses worth a second (and third) look.

Read your lease **before** signing, not the night of move-in, ideally with a 24-hour
cooling gap between "got it" and "signed it." You'll catch twice as much that way.

---

## 3.1 The five-minute version (before you go line by line)

Scan for these ten things; each maps to a section below:

1. Who's on it, what unit, what dates (Parties/Property/Term)
2. The rent number and any "other charges" (Rent & Fees)
3. When and how the deposit comes back (Deposit)
4. Late fee and grace period (Late Rent)
5. Who fixes what (Maintenance)
6. "Entry by landlord" rules (Landlord Entry)
7. Guests and pets clauses (Guests & Pets)
8. The break-lease / early-exit terms (Early Termination)
9. Renewal mechanics (Renewal)
10. Anything you *cannot* find (the missing-clause smell test)

---

## 3.2 Parties, property, and term

**What to check:**

- **Names spelled right.** Every adult living there must appear as a tenant. If your
  roommate signs and you don't, you have no lease rights. If they *leave* and you
  can't afford a solo rent... same clause, different crisis. (Chapter 5 has more.)
- **Unit address and unit number exact.** Match the listing you toured.
- **Term dates precise:** "beginning 1 June 2026, ending 31 May 2027." Watch for
  "month-to-month after one year" language — that's fine, but know that's the default
  if so. "Fixed term" vs "auto-renew" changes your exit math (Section 3.9).
- **Possession date** — the day you actually get keys — is that the same as the term
  start? (It usually is, but if the walkthrough is later, confirm.)

## 3.3 Rent and fees

- Is the number **per month** correct, and is anything whose amount was negotiable
  written in (concessions like "one month free" should appear, with math)?
- **What else you're billed for:** water, sewer, trash, gas, electric, internet,
  parking, storage, pet rent, "amenity" or "administrative" fees. Add it all up —
  this is the rent number from Chapter 1, now in contract form.
- **How rent is paid** (portal, check, autopay) and where payments are posted.
- **Prorated first partial month**, if any: how computed. (It should look sane: days
  of the month — or actual days — times daily rent.)

## 3.4 Security deposit

- The **amount** — often one month's rent, but varies by state (some states allow more,
  some cap it at one month's rent and no more).
- **Where it's held** (some states require it be held in a specific way, and/or pay
  interest).
- **Return timing and process** — states differ, often 14–45 days after move-out with
  an itemized statement. Whatever your state says, your lease usually mirrors or refines
  it. Chapter 7 is built on this clause: read that chapter with this paragraph in hand.
- **Automatic non-refundable deposits** are a red flag to clarify *why*.

## 3.5 Late fees and grace period

- A **grace period** ("rent due on the 1st, no fee until the 4th") — if present, circle
  it in your calendar forever. If absent, treat the 1st at midnight as real.
- **Fee amounts** — flat or daily-accelerating. Extreme per-day late fees are where
  state law often steps in; ask what local rules allow.
- Whether a late fee is **independent** of rent on the ledger (i.e., a "rent" payment
  that covers late fee but not full rent still counts as a short payment).

## 3.6 Maintenance, repairs, and who fixes what

Look for a split like "Landlord maintains major systems and structure" vs "Tenant
maintains cleanliness, minor items like filters and bulbs." Things worth asking:

- **Repair request process:** to whom, what form (email? portal?), and is there any
  timeline expected (some states require "reasonable time" standards for essential
  services; your resource is local law and your records — Chapter 6).
- **Failure-to-report cost shift:** a clause making *you* financially responsible for
  damage that worsened because you didn't report it. That makes reporting urgent for
  financial reasons — Chapter 6's paper trail again.
- **HVAC/pet-deposit conflicts, carpets and normal wear:** the phrase "normal wear and
  tear" shows up here; that phrase does heavy lifting in Chapter 7.

## 3.7 Landlord entry

Most leases give the landlord a right to enter with **notice** (often "24-hour
notice, reasonable times"), for repairs, showings, inspections, and emergencies.
Check:

- What counts as **emergency entry** (no notice usually — fire/flood/hazard).
- **Notice method** — email counts? Portal message? Text counts as notice?
- **Showing-before-move-out** rules: they can show your home during your last month —
  house rules (how much notice, always accompanied) go here. Many states require
  reasonable notice; know what *yours* says.

## 3.8 Guests and pets

- **Guest limits** ("more than 14 days in 6 months = moved in without approval") —
  clarify before your long-distance partner books flights.
- **Pet clause:** species/breed/weight limits, pet deposit vs pet *rent* (different
  money), whether the deposit is refundable, service/assistance animal handling. If
  you have an assistance animal, disability-related accommodations practically never
  count as "pets" — but handle it through the leasing office's process, in writing,
  early.
- **Smoking/coin/settings clauses** if relevant to you (cannabis often inherits the
  smoking clause even where legal for use in the state; buildings differ).

## 3.9 Breaking the lease / early termination

Everyone plans to finish the term; life laughs at plans anyway. Read this clause
*hopefully never needing it*:

- Does the lease describe an **early termination fee** or a "buy out" amount?
- **Assignment/sublet** rules — can you sublet or find a replacement tenant with
  landlord consent (often "consent not unreasonably withheld" — in some places, law
  steps in)? A replacement-tenant option is way cheaper than a fee.
- **Military/specific-law carve-outs:** federal law (e.g., the Servicemembers Civil
  Relief Act) and many states give special break rights in defined situations — flag
  where your life plausibly intersects these; ask before signing, not after.
- **Joint liability:** important if you have roommates. If any tenant stops paying,
  the landlord typically can demand **the full rent** from anyone and everyone on the
  lease. There's no "my half" as far as the landlord's ledger is concerned. Plan with
  a roommate agreement (Chapter 5), not hope.

## 3.10 Renewal and rent increases

- **How renewal happens** — offer letter X days prior, or automatic month-to-month?
- **Rent-increase notice period** — a month-to-month tenancy often requires advance
  written notice of increase (duration varies by location); longer fixed terms lock
  rent but lock you too. Neither setting is wrong — just know which you bought.

## 3.11 Rules, conduct, and the rest-of-the-binder clauses

Leases often point to **house rules/community guidelines** as if they're part of the
lease. Grab those documents too — parking, noise, common areas, and the list of "violations" they'll use. Read them once; you're
saving yourself future notices.

Also check the accident/insurance part:

- **Renter's insurance requirement:** minimum liability coverage often required, and
  they'll want proof. Get this before move-in day (it's ~$15/mo; see Chapter 1).
- **Insurance minimum limits** and whether they must list the landlord/company as
  "interested party." Every portal handles that with one toggle.

## 3.12 The missing-clause smell test

The scariest clauses are sometimes the *absent* ones. Before signing, you should know
the local default for anything the lease doesn't cover:

- **What happens on landlord non-repair** (local "repair and deduct" or
  report-to-authorities rules differ wildly).
- **Deposit return rules** (state timelines/statements).
- **Notice required to end a month-to-month** (often 30 days either side — but check).
- **Any required written lease** or **lease registration** in your area.

Gaps aren't automatically suspicious — but a landlord who'll say "yeah, whatever, we
don't really do that" to a normal ask deserves someone (you) who reads the whole thing
twice.

## 3.13 Red-flag clauses to question *before* signing

If you see these, ask:

- **"Landlord may enter at any time, for any reason."** (Notify/notice norm absent.)
- **"Tenant waives rights to [any statutory process]."** Waivers of laws usually can't
  hold up — and asking shows you read it.
- **"Deposit is non-refundable."** Then it's a fee. Ask why it's not called a fee.
- **"All changes by landlord to attached rules apply automatically."** Sounds fine;
  clarify whether you get notice of changes you're bound by.
- **Fee-stacking:** application + admin + move-in + *non-refundable "security"* +
  pet + amenity + parking + "processing" — ask for one written list of every charge,
  and take that list home.

You're not being difficult by asking. You're being the tenant they're glad they have.

## 3.14 Before you sign: the 60-second paper sanity check

- [ ] Can state the rent, deposit, term dates, and fee list without looking
- [ ] Every adult who'll live there is a named tenant (or is explicitly planned o/w)
- [ ] Repair process known and you've tested you can email/portal-message it
- [ ] Insurance ordered and proof ready if required
- [ ] Copy saved (photo/PDF) of the *signed* lease + any addenda + house rules
- [ ] One sentence in your notes app: "Move-in date is ___, walkthrough booked for ___"

Then sign. Chapter 4 is about the day you first hold those keys.
