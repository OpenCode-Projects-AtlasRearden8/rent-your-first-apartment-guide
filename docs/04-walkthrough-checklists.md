# Chapter 4: Walkthrough Checklists (Before You Sign, Before You're Stuck)

There are two walkthroughs in your rental life, and people only remember one of them:

1. **The apartment-tour walkthrough** (decide if you want to live there)
2. **The move-in walkthrough** (protect your money from day one)

Both matter. The second one determines whether you get your deposit back (Chapter 7).

> **Rule of the manual, stated here and everywhere: take photos. Room-by-room photos
> with a date visible, saved where you'll still have them in a year** (cloud backup,
> not just the phone you'll lose in 14 months).

---

## 4.1 Walkthrough #1 — The Apartment Tour

You get maybe 15 minutes with the person holding the keys. This list makes them count.
Bring your phone; take pictures even at the tour.

### Kitchen

- [ ] Turn on the faucet; check hot water arrives and drains fast (slow drain = singing
      noises from the plumbing soon).
- [ ] Open fridge and oven doors. Serious, visible grime = didn't clean = nice smell
      stayed anyway? Ask.
- [ ] Turn on every burner one click. Ask if the unit is gas (blue flame) or electric.
- [ ] Under the sink: look for water stains, dampness, smell of mildew.
- [ ] Run the dishwasher if there is one (or ask when it was last serviced). Listen
      for the drain cycle.

### Bathroom

- [ ] Flush the toilet. Two flushes if you're thorough. Watch the refill (it should
      stop on its own).
- [ ] Run the shower at full hot. Check pressure and that it doesn't die when the
      sink runs.
- [ ] Check the caulk/base of the tub: black spots = mold conversation.
- [ ] Run the exhaust fan. No fan? Bigger mildew risk — ask about it honestly.

### Every room

- [ ] Windows open *and* lock. Every one. Locked windows are a safety matter.
- [ ] Light switches work on every fixture.
- [ ] Outlets: bring a phone charger and test a couple (especially GFCI near water).
- [ ] Look *up*: ceiling stains (rings, bows) = leak history. Look *down*: soft spots.
- [ ] Doors: open, close, lock (bedroom lock indicates who gets privacy, though some
      places make them keyless; note what's what).

### Building & location

- [ ] Hallways/common areas: clean? lights working? (This tells you maintenance
      standards.)
- [ ] Laundry on-site? Try to see the laundry room; ask cost per load.
- [ ] Parking: assigned spot or street lottery?
- [ ] Walk the block at the time you'd normally arrive home. Noise, lighting,
      transit, and "would I walk here at 10pm" are all answers, not vibes.
- [ ] Cell reception in the unit (text from inside the farthest room).

### Questions worth asking while keys are in hand

- "What's the average **utility bill** here?" (A real answer exists.)
- "Who do I contact for **repairs**, and does that go through an app/email?"
- "**Heat** is what type — building-wide radiator, gas furnace, electric?" (Penny and
  comfort implications differ.)
- "What changed **between tenants** recently?" (New carpet? Replaced water heater?)
- "Are there any **planned** construction or fee changes I should know about?"

## 4.2 Walkthrough #2 — The Move-In Inspection (this is the money one)

Do this **before or immediately on possession day, in writing, with a copy kept.**
Many leases include a move-in condition form — if they offer one, use it *fully*. If
they don't, make your own from this list, sign it, photograph it, and email it to the
landlord with "please keep for the file" energy.

**Method:** one room at a time, everything photographed, every defect *written down*,
no defect too small. Walls are the deposit battleground; be specific ("2-inch scratch,
left of upper cabinet in kitchen, 1 ft above counter," not "kitchen wall scratched").

### Kitchen checklist

- [ ] Countertops (burns, chips, stains) — photo each visible one
- [ ] Cabinets inside and out, including hardware (loose hinges, warped doors)
- [ ] Appliances run (fridge ice/water line, oven light, range burners) — plug "all
      appliances present and working" into the form
- [ ] Sink, faucet, sprayer, disposal (smooth, no leak under sink)
- [ ] Floors (tiles cracked? vinyl lifting? carpet seams/stains)
- [ ] Walls/ceiling scuffs, nail holes, grease marks above the stove

### Bathroom checklist

- [ ] Toilet: seat, lid, bolt covers, doesn't run after refill
- [ ] Tub/shower: caulk condition, grout, drain speed, chips, rust at drain
- [ ] Sink + vanity: slow drain? loose faucet? cabinet inside
- [ ] Mirror/medicine cabinet doors
- [ ] Exhaust fan actually moves air (tissue test)
- [ ] Walls + ceiling for stains near the shower

### Bedrooms & living areas

- [ ] Walls/ceiling/floors: holes, stains, pet or smoke odor — *smell* each room.
      Odors that are "baked in" must be documented now, not at move-out.
- [ ] Closet: doors, rods, shelf
- [ ] Windows: open/close/lock, screen present/intact, blinds intact (missing slats,
      bent slats, listed per window)
- [ ] Doors: hardware, keys for all locks handed over (count them)
- [ ] Smoke detector + CO detector present and tested (press test button — this is a
      safety check, and their presence may be a legal requirement in your area)
- [ ] Outlets/switches/lights work in every room

### Entry, systems, and misc

- [ ] Entry door: peephole, lock(s), deadbolt, chain; front door key + mailbox key +
      building/gate fob(s) — *get every access item promised and note count*
- [ ] Thermostat: turns on, reads sanely; ask who controls heat
- [ ] Water heater location, no leaking at base
- [ ] HVAC filter: present/clean? (Knowing who supplies filters saves $)
- [ ] Garbage/recycling arrangements and day of week
- [ ] Parking spot number written down
- [ ] Star the take-off points: meter readings (electric/water) if you have a meter
      and can see it — photo of the readout with the date

### The documentation habit (30 minutes, saves hundreds)

1. **Take wide photos and close-ups** of every defect (wide shot so the location is
   identifiable, close shot so the defect is visible).
2. **Use timestamps:** most phones embed them; some people hold a dated note in frame.
3. **Email photos to yourself and the landlord** the same day. Subject line: "Move-in
   condition photos — [address], [date]." Download/store a copy folder named for the
   apartment. This folder is your Chapter 7 evidence.
4. **Ask for a signed copy** of any condition form. If the office is swamped, note in
   your email that the form/photos are "the condition record unless you send a
   correction by [date]." Then *actually* get the acknowledgment — even a "ok, got
   it" reply counts. Unacknowledged records are weaker at dispute time.

## 4.3 The not-in-the-checklist walkthrough

Two things Photos Can't Do, people forget both:

1. **The noise walkthrough** — stand in the unit at quiet hour. Then ask the person
   above/below what they hear. Road noise, train line, neighbor's subwoofer: all
   learned in five minutes of standing still, all invisible in photos.
2. **The "morning of the second day" walkthrough** — after your first night and hot
   shower + laundry + dishes ran, re-scan for leaks, weird smells, breaker tripping.
   Report anything within 48 hours in writing. Early reports = early fixes = no
   blame landing on you later.
