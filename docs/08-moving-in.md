# Chapter 8: Moving In (Logistics, Utilities, and the First Week)

You're approved, you've signed (Chapter 3), and the walkthrough is planned (Chapter 4).
Now it's the actual move. This chapter is the "how does a person physically do this"
one — utilities, packing, the first week, and the moment the apartment actually feels
like yours.

---

## 8.1 The two-week countdown

**T-minus ~14 days:**

- [ ] Utilities scheduled (Section 8.2) — activation on *morning of* move-in, since
      you'll need light and hot water on day one.
- [ ] Renter's insurance effective **on the 1st day of the lease**, not "this weekend."
- [ ] Book movers or van: end-of-month Saturday is peak pricing; a weekday mid-month
      is dramatically cheaper if you can swing it.
- [ ] Start the "don't move it" purge: donate/sell what you haven't used since the
      last season change. Every box you don't pack is money and back saved.
- [ ] Order the boring kit (Section 8.5) so it arrives before you do.

**T-minus ~7 days:**

- [ ] Confirm walkthrough/key-pickup time in writing.
- [ ] Pack by *room* and *frequency*: "first open" boxes (bedding, shower curtain,
      chargers, 2 days of clothes, coffee setup, paper plates) labeled loud.
- [ ] Label every box with room + 3-word contents. Future-you, unpacking at midnight,
      will remember this kindness.
- [ ] File a forwarding address with the post office; update address on **bank,
      employer, subscriptions, ID/license (as your state's rules require/timing),**
      and everywhere auto-delivered boxes ship.
- [ ] Defrost fridge (if moving yours) 24h ahead; drain the hose. Empty your
      current trash cans on the last day (movers moving your trash = discover comedy).

**Day before:**

- [ ] Charges packed for everything packed (nothing's worse than one cord in a
      mystery box's future, so: cords that go with a device travel *with* the device).
- [ ] Cash for tips + honest snacks for helpers; water.
- [ ] Big-items disassembly plan (or confirm movers disassemble).

## 8.2 Utilities, one list at a time

Order matters: some activations need a day's lead, some (cable internet) want a week.

| Utility | Lead time | Cost shape |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | 1–3 business days | Deposit may apply with no credit history; usually small |
| Water/sewer | Often handled by landlord or city; confirm who | Sometimes bundled into rent |
| Gas (heat/cooking) | 1–3 business days | Activation cap; someone may need to be home |
| Internet | **1–2 weeks** (installation appointments) | The long pole; schedule FIRST; ask about self-install kits |
| Trash/recycling | Usually via landlord/city | Confirm the pickup day |

Bring to each call/signup: the full address incl. unit #, your lease's start date,
your SSN/birthday for the credit-lite check (or ask about no-SSN deposit options),
and a payment card. Web swaps work for all four above; you'll want one city-portal
login for the water/trash things.

**Pro tips specific to first-time renters:**

- Put gas/electric/water accounts in **your name as it appears on the lease**.
- Ask for the **average bill for the unit** — again — because winter gas averages
  that three months fan-and-window-summer utilities budget never taught you.
- Don't cancel the old address's utilities until the move-out walkthrough is done
  (light and heat for cleaning and final photos = ergonomics and evidence).

## 8.3 Packing the apartment (the 8-box sane method)

Resistance is futile unless you do it by *category of life*, not by drawer:

1. **Bed** (sheets, pillows, blanket — goes ON the bed first night)
2. **Bath** (towels, shower curtain + rings, toilet paper, soap)
3. **Kitchen basics** (one pot, one pan, plates/bowls/cups for 4, utensils, knife,
   cutting board, dish soap, sponge, coffee)
4. **Chargers + electronics** (labeled cords as per 8.1)
5. **Clothes — 3 days** (plus laundry basket = free box)
6. **Medications + documents** (lease copy, ID, insurance info — *travels with you*,
   never on the truck)
7. **Tools** (screwdriver, box cutter, tape, light bulbs, plunger — location: one
   labeled box, then, congratulations, it's in the kitchen holding pens like every
   toolbox in history)
8. **Everything else** packed room-by-room, heavy bottom / light top, full but
   not bulging (full boxes stack; half-full ones crush)

Furniture pads and straps: borrow or rent the $20 kit; your backs and the walls
both benefit. Photograph the exit (current place) *after* loading: proof-of-clean
helps the *old* deposit (Chapter 7 applies in both directions!).

## 8.4 Move-in day, hour by hour

- [ ] Morning: utilities on (you scheduled activation by 9am — or confirm it
      happened); entry/fob access confirmed.
- [ ] Walkthrough **before** anything comes in (Chapter 4.2 — this IS the money
      walkthrough; don't do it "later").
- [ ] Then heavy furniture in first (it determines room arrangement); boxes after;
      "day-one boxes" last-on-truck, first-off, inside the door.
- [ ] Shield the corners: a couple of flattened boxes cut into corner Ls protects
      hallway paint (yours *and* the building's, which is deposit-adjacent).
- [ ] Photograph the truck-empty state for the return trip. Load out = clean out.
- [ ] Keys count: you should walk out of the office/landlord meeting with a photo
      of every key/fob and the count ("4 keys, 2 fobs, 1 mailbox") in your log.

## 8.5 The first-week setup kit (buy once, stop thinking)

The "new place tax" — things every first apartment needs the first week:

- Shower curtain **+ liner** + rings; bath mat; towels (2/person, at least).
- Toilet paper, hand soap, dish soap, sponges, trash bags (check the bin size!),
  paper towels, all-purpose cleaner, plunger.
- Light bulbs (walk each fixture; ceiling fixtures love weird sizes you don't own),
  possibly 1 extension cord + 1 power strip.
- Kitchen: salt/pepper/oil/cooking spray, coffee or tea situation, 4-paper-towels'
  worth of paper towels, and jar-opener (you'll know when).
- Curtains/rods if the unit is bare (bedrooms especially — sleep is infrastructure).
- Door mat (in = less floor filth = the clean in Chapter 7).
- Bed frame or at least: mattress fully off the floor is *safer* (ventilation, and
  no mattress-on-carpet mildew).

## 8.6 The first-week checklist (new address card filed)

- [ ] Test every breaker label (flipping breakers to verify the map) — do it once
      so the "which breaker is the bathroom" mystery is solved at noon, not midnight.
- [ ] Update address: bank + cards, employer/payroll, phone plan, insurance, ID
      as applicable, subscriptions, and your voter registration (many states make
      this easy online).
- [ ] Change the locks question: some leases/buildings re-key between tenants —
      *ask in writing* whether it was done. Don't DIY lock changes (Ch. 3.11).
- [ ] Meet the neighbors you share a wall/ceiling/floor with. Thirty seconds each:
      pulse on noise norms, someone to text about "is your water off too?", and
      the best parking for moving anything heavier than groceries.
- [ ] Find the mail room, trash chute, recycling, storage unit, and laundry
      machine app/coins. Ten minutes saves a week of friction.
- [ ] Test smoke and CO detectors (button) — confirm working *after* move-in too.
- [ ] Fire extinguisher: has one been provided? If the building requires one in-unit
      per your lease, get it; otherwise a small kitchen-rated one is $25 of quiet
      confidence.

## 8.7 The emotional part (yes, there's one paragraph)

The first week in a new apartment is loud with silence: no roommates/parents
rattling around, every bill in your name, and a fridge that starts impressively
empty. That's normal. It resolves on day 4, day 10 at the latest, in the specific
moment you realize you know where the breaker box is and *like* how the morning
light comes in. Until then: make the bed first, keep one candle or plant or soft
light on in the evening, and unpack the "first open" boxes before the "everything
else" boxes even if the everything-else boxes are judging you from the hallway.

You did the math, you read the lease, you photographed the walkthrough. You *are*
the person this manual was for. Welcome home.
