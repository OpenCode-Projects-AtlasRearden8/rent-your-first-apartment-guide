# Chapter 9: Glossary — Rental Words in Plain English

A quick reference. Definitions here are practical, not legal definitions — for the
legally binding meaning of a term in your location, consult your lease and your
local tenant resources.

---

**A**

**Addendum / Addenda** — Extra pages added to the base lease (pet rules, storage,
HOA rules). They're part of your contract; read them like menu items you're paying
for.

**Administration fee** — A non-refundable charge for paperwork. Ask what it covers;
sometimes it's negotiable; often it's not; know it before move-in, not after.

**Amenity fee** — A monthly or one-time charge for building perks (gym, lounge).
Math it against what you'd actually use, like Chapter 1 taught.

**Application fee** — Charge for processing your application (credit/background).
Often per adult applicant; often capped in some areas; ask the amount up front.

**Applicant screening** — The formal name for the credit/background/income review.
Landlords with written screening criteria must typically follow them; ask to see
"the criteria."

**Assignment (lease assignment)** — Transferring your whole lease to someone else.
Different from subletting (you keep some connection). Your lease's rules on both
live in the early-termination clause.

**Assistance animal** — An animal providing disability-related support. Not legally
a "pet" in most housing contexts; accommodation requests go through the leasing
office in writing, early. (Service animals are a trained-animal category with
separate rules.)

**B**

**Break the lease** — Ending the term early. Costs and processes are in the lease's
termination clause (Ch. 3.9); the cheapest version is usually replacing yourself.

**C**

**Co-tenant** — Someone on the lease with you. You're each responsible for the
*whole* rent, jointly — the landlord doesn't split you into halves. (Chapter 5
exists for this exact reason.)

**Cosigner / Guarantor** — Someone signing to promise payment if you don't. Big
commitment for them; common and normal for first apartments.

**Condition form** — Move-in/move-out inspection sheet recording the unit's state.
Your deposit's best friend. Chapter 4.2 is how to fill one to survive Chapter 7.

**Credit check** — Formal review of your credit history by the landlord/screening
service. Usually a soft-ish pull for rentals; your written permission is required.

**D**

**Deposit (security deposit)** — Money you give the landlord, held against damage
and unpaid rent. It's *your money* until legitimately offset — Chapter 7 is about
getting it back with the receipts to prove it.

**Deposit alternative** — Newer products (insurance-backed, monthly small fees)
replacing full deposits; read their terms like a lease, because they are one.

**E**

**Early termination clause** — The lease paragraph about leaving before the term
ends. Read it *hopefully never* but read it (Ch. 3.9).

**Eviction** — The legal process by which a tenant is removed. It's a court thing, *not* a landlord-locks-the-door thing
 *not* a landlord-locks-the-door thing (landlords cannot self-help evict in most
places), and it has serious long-term rental-housing consequences. If you get
court papers, take them seriously; local legal aid is the right resource.

**F**

**Fair housing** — Laws prohibiting discrimination in housing (race, color,
religion, national origin, sex, familial status, disability, and many more
categories under state/local law). Applies to landlords, listings, and screening;
violations have real remedies. If you suspect discrimination, your local fair
housing agency handles it.

**Fixed-term lease** — Lease for a set period (e.g., 12 months) with defined start
and end, usually requiring notice to not renew. Contrast month-to-month.

**Furnished / unfurnished** — What's included. "Furnished" varies from furniture to
(potentially) kitchenware — get the list, in writing, whatever the listing says.

**G**

**Grace period** — Days after due date with no late fee (e.g., "fee starts on the
4th"). Not universal; mark your calendar to the *fee* date, not the due date, if
your lease has one.

**H**

**Habitability** — The requirement that housing be fit to live in (working heat,
water, electricity, safe structure...). If essentials fail, local law has
protections and processes; the manual's role is records (Chapter 6), not legal
strategy.

**Holdover** — Staying past your lease's end date without a renewal. Usually
month-to-month sometimes with a premium; sometimes a violation. Know which, and
renew (or move) deliberately, never accidentally.

**House rules** — Building rules the lease usually makes binding. Part of the
document family you should have a copy of, with the lease.

**I**

**Inspection, move-in/move-out** — The walkthroughs of Chapters 4.2 and 7.3. The
most misunderstood pair of meetings in all of renting.

**Interest on deposit** — Some states require deposits earn interest, paid to the
tenant. Local question; ask, don't assume either way.

**J**

**Joint and several liability** — Lawyer-phrase for "any of you can be billed the
whole amount." The co-tenant reality of Chapter 5.3. Boring words, big stakes.

**L**

**Late fee** — Charge for rent paid after the grace period. Amounts and caps vary
by location; your lease states yours.

**Lease (rental agreement)** — The contract. Also the name for receiving a copy —
which you must always have: your signed copy, saved as PDF, within reach for a year.

**Listing (rental listing)** — The ad. Where scams (Ch. 2.4) and too-good rents
live; also where your future home lives. Reading both ways is a skill.

**M**

**Master tenant** — One person on the lease; others are their subtenants/roommates.
Chapter 5.1's option 2.

**Month-to-month** — Tenancy renewing monthly with (usually) 30-day notice either
side, per local rules. Flexible; rent can rise at renewal with proper notice.

**Move-in date / possession** — The day keys transfer and the lease says you may
occupy. Your utilities and insurance activate *this* day, not the visit day.

**N**

**Notice (to vacate / to enter / to increase)** — Formal written warning of an
upcoming something. Different notices have different required lead times in
different places; your lease + local rules set them. When in doubt, send it in
writing *early* and keep proof.

**Normalization, deposit** — (term used here, not in law): the process of itemizing
deductions from the deposit at move-out, per your state's statement rules.

**O**

**Occupant (approved)** — Someone living there who isn't a lease tenant. Rights
depend entirely on the lease + your roommate agreement.

**Ordinance/Statute (local)** — City/state law specific to rentals. Often the
actual source of deposit deadlines, notice periods, and repair duties. When a lease
falls silent, the local statute is the default rulebook.

**P**

**Pet deposit vs pet rent** — One-time money vs monthly money. Different pots,
different refundability, sometimes both charged. The lease should say which and how
much for each.

**Possession (legal)** — Your right to occupy the unit per the lease. Landlord
entry rules show respect for it (Ch. 3.7).

**Prorated rent** — Adjusted first/last-month rent for partial periods. Should equal
daily rate × days; check the math appears sane.

**R**

**Reasonable notice** — The landlord's obligation to warn you before entering
(often 24–48 hours, varies). "Reasonable" is defined by law and lease, not vibes.

**Renewal** — Agreeing to another term. Watch for the offer letter deadline and
rent-increase notice — Chapter 3.10.

**Renters' insurance** — Covers your stuff and liability, ~$12–30/month, usually
required by lease. Get it day one; it's the cheapest adult-self-care money in this
manual.

**S**

**Security deposit** — see Deposit.

**Screening criteria** — The landlord's written approval standards (income ratio,
credit floor, etc.). Ask for it; applicants who *match a written standard* argue
from paper, not feelings.

**Sublet / Sublease** — You rent to someone else while remaining responsible to the
landlord. Requires landlord consent per most leases and local rules; do it through
the process, not informally.

**T**

**Tenant screening report ** — Also called "resident screening:" the compiled
credit/background/eviction report. If it contains errors, the reporting company has
legal duties to correct them in many places; disputes go through them in writing.

**Term** — How long the lease runs. "Fixed term" vs "month-to-month" changes
everything downstream (exit rights, renewal, increase mechanics).

**U**

**Utilities** — Electricity, gas, water/sewer, trash, internet. The "who pays
which, and how billed" answer should be *a list, in writing* before you sign
(Chapter 1.3 does the math; Chapter 8.2 does the setup).

**V**

**Verification** — The landlord confirming your stated income/employment with
documents. This is why Chapter 2 says "round income down, never up."

**Walkthrough** — see Inspection; the verb form in this manual is always "with
photos."

**W**

**Wear and tear (normal)** — Deterioration from ordinary use over time: faded
paint, slightly scuffed hallway floor. Chapter 7.6's fair-deduction test. Landlords
may not charge you for normal wear; they may charge for damage you caused — the
line between them is exactly what your move-in photos drew.
