# Renting Your First Apartment: Start Here

*The manual that talks to you like a friend would.*

---

## Who this is for

You, probably. If you're about to rent your first real place — or you're helping someone
who is — this manual is for you. It doesn't assume you have a lawyer, a landlord on speed
dial, or a parent who already knows this stuff. It assumes you have a phone, a bank
account, and a healthy amount of "wait, what does that word mean?"

## How to use this manual

1. **Read it in order the first time.** The chapters build on each other. Budgeting
   comes first because it decides everything else.
2. **Come back with the checklists.** The walkthrough and move-out chapters are meant to
   be used with a pen (or the printable PDFs from the `site/` pages).
3. **Skim the glossary whenever a lease word confuses you.** That's what it's there for.

## The one big idea

Renting works so much better when you write things down. Every chapter comes back to the
same theme: **memories are fuzzy, records are not.** Photographs before move-in. A
paper trail for every repair. A dated note for every agreement with a landlord or a
roommate. You will save yourself real money — often hundreds or thousands of dollars —
just by being the tenant who has receipts.

## What this manual is *not*

- **It is not legal advice.** No chapter in this manual is legal advice, and nothing in
  it creates a lawyer–client relationship. It's an educational guide written in plain
  English.
- **It is not specific to your state or country.** Renting rules vary a lot by
  location — deposit timelines, notice periods, what a landlord must disclose. Some
  places cap late fees; some don't. Some require interest on deposits; some don't.
  Where the law matters (deposits, evictions, repairs), this manual tells you what
  questions to ask and where reliable local answers usually live: your state or city's
  official housing/tenant resources, legal-aid organizations, and the actual text of
  your lease.
- **It is not a guarantee.** Every landlord, building, and market is different. Use
  this as a framework and good-sense starting point, not a script.

When a chapter says "check your local rules," that's the manual being honest with you —
that's the part where a real local expert beats a general guide every time.
