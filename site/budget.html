1 · The Budget Worksheet

Companion to Chapter 1 (docs/01-budget-real-math.md). Print one; keep it until you've lived somewhere a full quarter.

Guideline, not law or financial advice. Percentages below are common starting rules of thumb; adapt them to your life and your local cost of living.

A. Monthly budget (use your LOW-income month, not your average)

  • Monthly take-home pay (after taxes): $ ____________
  • Second job / gigs (realistic low number): $ ____________
  • Total monthly money in: $ ____________

Money out — housing (the REAL number)

  • Sticker rent: $ ____________
  • Renter's insurance: $ ____________
  • Electric (budget the worst season): $ ____________
  • Gas / heat: $ ____________
  • Water / sewer / trash: $ ____________
  • Internet: $ ____________
  • Parking / transit pass: $ ____________
  • Pet rent (if any): $ ____________
  • Housing total: $ ____________   (target ≤ ~40% of take-home / ≤ 30% of gross)

Money out — everything else

  • Groceries: $ ____________
  • Phone: $ ____________
  • Transport (beyond commuter pass above): $ ____________
  • Loan / card minimums: $ ____________
  • Fun money (keep some — you're a person): $ ____________
  • Other monthly total: $ ____________

The check

  • Money in − housing − everything else = $ ____________ left over
  • Is that number positive? If no, re-run this with a roommate (split it two ways) or a smaller unit before you tour anything.

B. Move-in bill calculator

Rule of thumb to save: 3 × monthly rent + $500.

  • First month's rent: $ ____________
  • Security deposit (1–2 × rent in many markets): $ ____________
  • Application fee(s), per adult: $ ____________
  • Admin / move-in / key fees: $ ____________
  • Pet deposit (if any): $ ____________
  • Renter's insurance first payment: $ ____________
  • Utility activation / deposits: $ ____________
  • Moving help (movers, van rental, pizza economy): $ ____________
  • Move-in total: $ ____________
  • Do I have this saved? (be honest — if not, that's a start-dating-tomorrow situation, not a deal)

A printable reminder of the two money-rules that matter: keep one full month of the real number saved as an emergency cushion, and rent comes first among bills if a month goes sideways.
